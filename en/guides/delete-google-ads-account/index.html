Deleting a Google Ads account — cancellation, data, leftover credit

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 10 August 2026

You cannot really delete a Google Ads account — Google offers cancellation. Ads stop within 24 hours, you can still sign in afterwards, accrued costs still have to be paid, and unused credit is normally refunded within 4 to 12 weeks. A cancelled account can be reactivated at any time.

Search for how to delete a Google Ads account and you mostly find step-by-step guides — many of them still describing the old menu route through the tools icon and Setup → Preferences. In the current Google Ads interface the path sits under Admin → Account settings. What those guides rarely cover is what happens after cancellation to your data, your leftover credit and the account itself. This guide uses Google's own help pages only.

Cancel or delete — what Google actually offers

There is no button in Google Ads that permanently removes an account. The action is called cancel my account. That is not a wording quibble: the account continues to exist afterwards, it is simply inactive.

Google describes the immediate effect plainly: "All your ads will stop within 24 hours." (Google Ads Help)

And about the account itself: "After you cancel, you'll still be able to access the data in your Google Ads account by logging in." (Google Ads Help)

So campaign data, conversion history and invoices are not gone the moment you cancel.

How to cancel a Google Ads account

The click path in the current interface:

StepWhere
1Open the Admin menu
2Go to Account settings
3Open the Account status drop-down
4Select Cancel my account

Source: Google Ads Help on cancelling your account. If a guide tells you to go through Tools and settings → Setup → Preferences instead, it is describing an older version of the interface.

Do you still owe money after cancelling?

Yes. Cancelling does not wipe costs you have already run up: "If you cancel your account, you'll still need to pay for any accrued costs. You can only receive a refund after the account has been officially canceled." (Google Ads Help)

That order is the part most guides leave out — settle what you owe first, then cancel, then the refund follows. Not the other way round.

How long does the refund take?

You do not have to request it: "A refund for any unused funds in your account (excluding promotional credits) will be automatically processed." (Google Ads Help)

The headline timeframe: "You will generally receive your refund within 4-12 weeks." (Google Ads Help) Within that range, Google separates it by payment method:

Payment methodRefund processing time
Credit card2–3 weeks
Bank transfer3–4 weeks
SEPA (direct debit)8 weeks

Source: Google Ads Help on refunds. For credit cards, the card company's own processing time comes on top of that. If you pay by SEPA direct debit — common across Europe — plan for eight weeks rather than four.

Can you reactivate the account later?

Yes, with no deadline attached: "You'll always be able to reactivate your canceled account." (Google Ads Help) Guides that claim a 24-hour window for reactivation are not reflecting Google's documentation.

There is one condition once you are back: "After reactivation, if the account doesn't have any activity and doesn't serve any ads for 3 months, then the account will be automatically canceled again." (Google Ads Help) Reactivating an account just in case, without running ads, puts you back in the same position three months later.

Accounts also cancel themselves

The point almost no guide mentions: you do not have to do anything for an unused account to go away. Google cancels it for you — the notice reads: "Your Google Ads account has been inactive for more than 15 months with no spending and was automatically canceled." (Google Ads Help)

So if your goal is simply to retire an account you no longer use, there is nothing to click. The reverse also holds: an account you want to keep needs some activity inside 15 months.

What happens to your data?

It does not stay forever. Google starts removing it roughly a month in: "In an effort to remove your data, the following will take place about a month after account cancelation" (Google Ads Help). The steps Google lists there include your remarketing and Customer Match lists, whose membership duration is shortened.

Practical consequence: if you need campaign structure, search terms, conversion data or invoices for bookkeeping or a later restart, export them before you cancel — not weeks afterwards.

When cancelling is the wrong move

Cancellation is right when you are finished with Google Ads. If you only want to stop the spend — seasonally, during a stock shortage, or until tracking is fixed — pausing your campaigns is the better route: the account stays active, the 15-month clock never runs out, and you keep your learning history and account structure.

If the real question is whether Google Ads pays off for your business at all, the maths beforehand helps more than the exit: see how much Google Ads costs and Google Ads on a small budget. If the channel is fine but the management is not, what you actually want is switching agencies — there the account stays exactly where it is.
