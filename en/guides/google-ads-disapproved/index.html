Google Ads disapproved — edit it or appeal it

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 13 August 2026

A disapproved ad does not run until the policy violation is fixed and the ad is reviewed again. There are exactly two routes back: edit the ad so it complies — which triggers an automatic re-review — or appeal the decision if you believe the review got it wrong.

Disapproval hits the individual ad, not the account — that is the key difference from a suspension. The rest of the campaign keeps running, which is exactly why it gets missed: the numbers slide without anything looking obviously broken.

What the status actually means

Google puts the consequence plainly: "A disapproved ad won't be able to run until the policy violation is fixed and the ad is reviewed again." (Google Ads Help)

That is two conditions, not one: fixed and reviewed again. A corrected ad does not resume instantly; it resumes after the new review.

The two routes back

You either edit the ad to comply with policy, or appeal the decision if you believe an error was made during review (Google Ads Help).

RouteWhen it fitsWhat happens
Edit the adThe violation makes sense to youAutomatic re-review
AppealYou think the decision is wrongThe decision gets re-examined

Editing triggers the review by itself

A detail that saves time: after a change you do not have to submit anything. "Your ad will automatically be reviewed again." (Google Ads Help)

It also means every edit restarts the review clock. Tweaking the same ad three times in a row extends the period where it is not running — better to fix it properly once than to guess three times.

Why disapprovals get missed

Because the account looks fine. Campaign enabled, budget in place, just one ad no longer serving. In an ad group with several ads the drop is invisible at first — the remaining ads absorb the traffic, usually at worse performance.

If a campaign stops producing impressions entirely, disapproval is only one of five possible causes. The order to check them in is in the no-impressions guide.

What we turn this into as a routine

  • Check ad status the day after any upload, rather than waiting for the numbers to fall.
  • On a disapproval, read the named policy violation before changing anything — guessing costs another review cycle.
  • On repeated disapprovals of the same kind, look at the offer or the landing page, not the ad text.
