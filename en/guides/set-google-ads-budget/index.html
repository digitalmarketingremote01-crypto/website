Setting a Google Ads budget — why $10 a day is not $300 a month

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 3 September 2026

The daily budget in Google Ads is an average, not a cap for any single day. On one day Google may spend up to twice it. Across the month it is capped at 30.4 times the daily budget. You never pay more than those limits — Google covers the difference.

The most common first-month shock: the daily budget says $10, and a Tuesday shows $18 in costs. That is not a fault, it is documented behaviour. Knowing it in advance changes how you plan — and stops the panicked call on day three.

Daily budget means average

Google defines it exactly that way: "Your average daily budget is the average amount that you set for each ad campaign on a per-day basis." (Google Ads Help)

The logic: on days with more relevant demand it can spend more, on quieter days less. It evens out across the month.

The two limits that actually apply

LimitSizeAt a $10 daily budget
Daily2× the daily budgetup to $20 in one day
Monthly30.4× the daily budget$304 for the month

In Google's words, the daily limit is "two times your average daily budget for most campaigns" and the monthly limit is "30.4 times your average daily budget for most campaigns" (Google Ads Help).

The 30.4 is not arbitrary — it is the average number of days in a month (365 ÷ 12).

What if Google overdelivers?

It happens, and it is not billed to you: "you'll never pay more than these two limits" — and: "When such a situation occurs, Google will cover the difference." (Google Ads Help)

Worth knowing when you audit your own account: what you see in the daily view is delivery cost. What reaches the invoice is capped. Confusing the two sends people hunting for faults that are not there.

The mid-month change trap

This is the part almost no guide mentions: "If you adjust your average daily budget during the month, the remainder of the month will pace to your new daily budget multiplied by the remaining calendar days in the month (not the remaining number of days the campaign is scheduled to run)." (Google Ads Help)

In practice: raising the budget on the 20th does not simply apply pro rata to what is left — it resets the calculation for the rest of the month. Adjust twice in one month and you have lost track of your own ceiling.

On top of that, budget changes sit in the bidding environment and can restart the learning period. Two good reasons to set the budget and then leave it alone.

How high should it be?

Not "as much as possible" — enough that the maths works: cost per click × clicks per enquiry × enquiries per sale has to stay under your margin. The honest calculation, including the floor below which it does not work, is in the small-budget guide, and real click prices are in the cost guide.

And when a budget is too small, it rarely shows up as slightly weaker performance — it often shows up as no delivery at all, see no impressions.
