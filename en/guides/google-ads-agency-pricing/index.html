Google Ads agency pricing: what agencies actually charge in 2026

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 5 August 2026

Google Ads agencies bill three ways: a flat monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend, or an hourly rate. US agencies that publish their own flat rates land between $549 and $1,000/month, percentages run 10–20% of spend (WebFX), and hourly rates cluster at $100–$149 (Clutch). UK agencies charge flat fees of £149–£1,500/month (Count SEO, Social Surge). We publish our own EUR price list from €199/month, month-to-month — see the full pricing.

Ask ten Google Ads agencies what they charge and most will quote you a range only after a call. This guide collects what's actually published: real price pages from named US and UK agencies, two independent survey sources, and our own price list. One distinction matters throughout — this is about the agency's management fee, not the ad spend that gets paid to Google. Those are two separate line items on every invoice, and mixing them up is the single most common way to misread an agency quote.

What are the three PPC pricing models?

Agencies price Google Ads management three main ways: a flat monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend, or an hourly rate — with a performance-based variant (a fixed amount per conversion) as a fourth, less common option. US agency Sagapixel names this structure directly on its own pricing page, and it matches what's visible across every other agency price page checked for this guide.

The percentage model carries a documented incentive problem. Sagapixel puts it bluntly: "They want you to increase your ad spend so that their revenue goes up." John Doherty, founder of the agency-matching marketplace Credo, goes further in a 2023 article that the company still hosts: "Incentives are misaligned from the jump. This rarely leads to a longlasting client-agency relationship." He adds: "As a business owner, you're incentivized to maximize every dollar of your spend and to spend as little as possible. As a digital agency, the goal is to get your clients to spend more, even if it may not in their best interest." That piece is three years old, but it's still the clearest independent statement of the conflict.

What minimum ad spend do you need?

There's no consensus figure, but the published numbers cluster. WebFX says there's "no strict minimum budget to run PPC ads" at all, while recommending $1,000–$3,000/month as the level that produces meaningful optimization data. Social Surge advertises no minimum ad spend requirement. Count SEO recommends spend starting at £300/month for its smallest tier, rising to £900/month for its largest. Emprise Digital's own fee structure creates an implicit inflection point at $10,000/month in spend, where its fee model switches entirely from flat-plus-spend to a straight 10% commission.

There's a structural reason some agencies set these thresholds where they do. Google's own documentation is specific about the conversion volume automated bidding needs to work reliably: Target ROAS bidding guidance requires "at least 15 conversions in the past 30 days at the conversion tracking level" for Search and Shopping campaigns, and Google's general Smart Bidding guidance recommends evaluating performance over periods with "at least 30 conversions... (50 conversions for Target ROAS)." Put plainly: if a monthly budget is too small to generate 15–50 conversions in a typical month for a given industry and conversion value, Smart Bidding has too little data to work as designed — no agency, however good, changes that math. If that's your situation, the honest answer is to say so rather than to sign a retainer anyway. How do you tell a fair offer from a bad one?

A few checks come straight out of the verified numbers above:

  • Month-to-month terms exist in this market — Third Marble Marketing advertises "Month to Month," and Social Surge runs on rolling monthly agreements. A long minimum term isn't something you have to accept.
  • If the fee is a percentage of spend, ask how the incentive conflict is handled. The structural problem is documented above (Doherty, Sagapixel) — a straight answer to that question tells you a lot.
  • Ask about setup and restructure fees before you sign. They vary widely: Logical Position charges a separate restructure fee ($349–$649 depending on contract length), Third Marble charges $399 up front, and Emprise charges $2,000 for its initial build. None of these are hidden on the agencies' own pages — they just aren't always mentioned on a first call.
  • Ask who owns the ad account before you sign. Campaigns should run in an account you own, with the agency working through access rights — so the account, its data and its history stay with you if the relationship ends. That is how we work, and it is a fair question to settle up front with anyone else.

Our own pilot term follows the same logic in the other direction: if the goals agreed for month one are missed, month two carries no management fee. That's a statement about how the first two months are structured, not a promise about results.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between an agency's fee and my ad spend? The management fee is what you pay the agency to run and optimize your account. Ad spend is separate money paid directly to Google or Meta for the ads themselves. Under a percentage model (Sagapixel, for example, charges 15–22% of spend) the fee is calculated from your spend, but it is still a separate cost on top of it.

Is a percentage of ad spend or a flat fee better? Neither is universally better — see the pricing-model table above. A flat fee is predictable; a percentage model scales with account size but has documented misaligned incentives once spend grows (Doherty, Credo, 2023). Hourly billing ($100–$149/hr per Clutch) suits well-defined, limited-scope work.

Do I need a minimum ad budget to hire an agency? Not according to WebFX or Social Surge, both of which state there's no strict minimum. But Google's own Smart Bidding guidance recommends at least 15–30 conversions a month for automated bidding to work reliably — below that, results are harder to optimize regardless of agency quality.

Are there setup fees on top of the monthly fee? Sometimes. Third Marble charges $399, Emprise Digital charges $2,000, and Logical Position charges a separate restructure fee. We don't charge one — see our full price list.

How much do UK PPC agencies charge compared to US agencies? The two markets aren't directly comparable since figures are in different currencies and this guide doesn't convert between them. UK flat fees published by Count SEO and Social Surge run £149–£2,250/month; the US agencies above publish flat fees between $549 and $1,000/month plus percentage and hybrid models, alongside a $100–$149/hr band on Clutch.
