Cancelling Google Ads — the three ways to stop the spend

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 13 August 2026

Google Ads has no contract term and no notice period. Three different actions get called cancelling: pause (stops the spend, keeps everything), remove (permanent, cannot be undone) and cancel the account (ads stop within 24 hours). For most situations pausing is the right one, not cancelling.

People who want to cancel Google Ads usually mean one of two things: stop the money going out — or leave altogether. Google treats these as three separate actions with very different consequences, and one of them cannot be reversed. This guide separates them properly, using Google's own help pages.

Is there a notice period?

No. Google Ads has no minimum term: you run ads as long as you want and stop when you want. On pausing, Google says: "You can pause your ads for any period of time that you'd like, and you can resume your ads when you want." (Google Ads Help)

There may still be a notice period — not with Google, but with your agency. Those are two separate contracts: the agency agreement covers the management work, while the Google Ads account belongs to you independently of it. How the handover works is covered in the guide to switching agencies.

Pausing: the normal case

Pausing is what most people are actually looking for. It stops the spend without giving anything up.

Google is unambiguous on cost: "Your ads won't accrue charges while they're paused." (Google Ads Help)

And on what survives: "By pausing your ad, you can save the ad for later without having to rewrite it and send it through review again." (Google Ads Help)

At campaign level Google describes it the same way: "This is temporary. It stops your ads from showing to audiences and prevents you from accruing further charges." (Google Ads Help)

The account stays active throughout. Structure, ads, keywords and historical data stay where they are — which matters if you restart later, because you are not beginning from zero.

One thing to check anyway: costs accrued before you paused are still billed. Pausing takes effect from the moment you pause, not retroactively.

Removing: the one with no way back

Removing sounds gentler than it is. Google calls it permanent and leaves no room for doubt: "You can't resume or restore it." (Google Ads Help) Pausing is the opposite: "You can resume the campaign at any time and all settings and history are preserved." (Google Ads Help)

And explicitly, on getting it back: "No, removal is permanent. To run that campaign again, you will need to create a new campaign or duplicate the settings of the removed campaign into a new one." (Google Ads Help)

The data is not gone, but it is frozen: "While you can't edit the settings or resume the campaign, you can still view its historical performance data." (Google Ads Help)

In practice: if you are unsure, pause. Removing is for campaigns you are certain you will never need again — and even then it saves you nothing on cost compared with pausing.

Cancelling the account: only for a real exit

The third option is not about individual campaigns but the whole account. It is the right step if you are done with Google Ads — with its own consequences for leftover credit, data and reactivation. The full process, including refund timelines, is in the guide to deleting a Google Ads account.

Short version: ads stop within 24 hours, accrued costs still have to be paid, you can still sign in, and a cancelled account can be reactivated at any time.

What we tell clients

When campaigns underperform, cancelling is rarely the fix — it is usually a targeting, budget or tracking problem. Whether your budget is viable at all is worked through honestly in the small-budget guide, and what Google Ads actually costs is in the cost guide.

If you want to stop because the management is not working: pause the campaigns, keep the account open, and change who runs it. The account is yours — not the agency's.
