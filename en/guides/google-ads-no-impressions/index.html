Google Ads with no impressions — the causes, in order

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 13 August 2026

Zero impressions almost always comes down to one of five things: the ad is still under review (usually 24–48 hours), the account is suspended or billing is not set up, bids are too low, the budget is too small — or targeting is too narrow. Check them in exactly that order.

Zero impressions is not an optimisation problem, it is an elimination problem: something is stopping the ad from entering an auction at all. The order below is deliberate — from causes you can rule out in minutes to ones that mean rebuilding something.

1. Is the ad still in review?

The most common cause on new campaigns is simply time: "Most ads are reviewed within 24-48 hours. However, for new accounts or specific formats like image or video, it can take up to 5 business days for review." (Google Ads Help)

Before that window is up, troubleshooting is wasted effort.

2. Is something wrong with the account or billing?

The second most common cause has nothing to do with the campaign: "If your account is suspended, under a security review, or if you have a billing issue on your account (such as billing hold or unconfigured payment method), you won't be able to run your ads until the problem is resolved." (Google Ads Help)

An expired card is enough to trigger this. If it is a genuine suspension, see the guide to suspended accounts.

3. Are the bids too low?

Low bids do not formally exclude you — they just keep you out of the auctions: "While it may be possible to serve ads with a very low bid, your campaign isn't likely to enter or win as many auctions and your ads may not have any impressions." (Google Ads Help)

This is where very small budgets actually fail — not on the budget itself, but on the bid that follows from it.

4. Is the budget too small?

"When you have a low budget, your ads may not serve as often while Google Ads ensures your campaign doesn't spend more than your spending limit" (Google Ads Help)

In expensive sectors with a very small daily budget, not as often can round down to nothing. The honest break-even maths is in the small-budget guide.

5. Is targeting too narrow?

"While targeting settings help you find the right users, overly restrictive geographic or audience targeting can significantly limit your reach." (Google Ads Help)

The classic pattern: one location, plus a narrow audience, plus a language filter, plus an ad schedule. Each restriction looks sensible alone; stacked together there is nobody left to show to.

What if you have impressions but no clicks?

That is a different problem with different causes — the ad reaches the auction but does not persuade. See the guide to getting no clicks. If the ad was disapproved, see the guide to disapproved ads.
