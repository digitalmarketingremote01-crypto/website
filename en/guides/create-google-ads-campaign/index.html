Creating a Google Ads campaign — and what must be ready first

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 15 August 2026

The path is short: Campaigns icon → Campaigns → plus button → New campaign. Then you pick the objective first and the campaign type second — Google proposes types based on the goal you chose. What decides the result, though, is not the click path but what is finished beforehand: tracking, landing page, budget and negative keywords.

Building a campaign takes ten minutes. Whether it earns anything is decided before that. This guide covers both: the documented process, and the preparation that step-by-step guides usually leave out.

The official click path

Google describes it as: "In your Google Ads account, click the Campaigns icon" — then through the section menu, and finally "then select New campaign". (Google Ads Help)

Objective first, type second

The order is not a formality: "Choose your campaign objective, then follow the prompts in Google Ads." (Google Ads Help)

The type then follows from it: "Select a campaign type. If you selected a goal, you'll be given the options for the best campaign types to achieve it." (Google Ads Help)

Picking the campaign type first out of habit and then back-filling a goal inverts the logic — and you get recommendations optimised toward the wrong purpose.

What should be ready before you launch

PreparationWhy beforehand
Conversion trackingNo conversions means no bid strategy can learn — see the learning period
Landing pageThe click has to arrive somewhere that delivers the ad's promise
A budget with maths behind itToo small a budget means bids too low to serve, and zero impressions
Negative keywordsSaves wasted spend from day one instead of cleaning it up later

You can create the account itself without launching a campaign at all — the documented route is in the account without a campaign guide. That is the right order: account, tracking, then campaign.

After you launch

Expect an ad review before anything runs, then the learning period before the numbers mean anything. Both are documented and both take longer than most people expect:

  • Ad review: usually 24–48 hours, longer on new accounts — details.
  • Learning period: up to 3 weeks or 1–2 conversion cycles — details.

Change as little as possible during that window: every adjustment to the bid strategy can restart the learning period.

When the effort is not worth it

If the maths does not work beforehand, a well-built campaign will not fix it. The honest break-even view is in the small-budget guide, and the real cost ranges in the cost guide. If both hold up, the rest is craft.
