Google Ads reporting — what a report has to contain

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 3 September 2026

Google Ads can send reports itself — scheduled by frequency and format, emailed to anyone with access. That settles the mechanics. The real question is the content: a report showing clicks and impressions but no enquiries and no cost per enquiry tells you nothing about your business.

Reporting rarely fails on the technology. It fails because the report shows numbers that look good instead of numbers that support a decision.

Google can send the reports for you

You need neither an agency nor a tool for this: "You can have Google Ads email a statistics table as a report to you and other people who have the direct access level to view your reports." (Google Ads Help)

You choose frequency and format: "Choose the frequency (examples: daily, weekly) that the reports are sent and in what format (for example, CSV and PDF)." (Google Ads Help)

And a view you have built once can be kept: "To save a copy of the table that you can edit in the Report Editor, click Save this report and enter a name." (Google Ads Help)

Granting the right access level for reports is covered in the access levels guide.

Which numbers actually matter

MetricWhat it answersWhy it is not enough alone
ImpressionsDid it serve?Says nothing about interest
Clicks / CTRIs the ad appealing?A click is not a customer
ConversionsAre enquiries arriving?Only meaningful with clean tracking
Cost per conversionWhat does an enquiry cost?The number that decides whether to continue
Search termsWhat did you pay for?See the search terms report

The only number that carries a business decision is cost per enquiry against what a customer is worth to you. Everything above it is context.

How to spot an empty agency report

Four warning signs, named honestly — even though we are an agency ourselves:

  1. Only clicks and impressions. Both almost always rise when you raise the budget. That is not a result.
  2. No cost per enquiry. Without it you cannot judge whether the channel pays.
  3. No search terms. Then you cannot see what your money actually bought.
  4. Charts but no decision. A report should say in one sentence what happens next and why.

If a report arrives monthly but never contains a recommendation, it is documentation, not management.

How often to report

Monthly for decisions, weekly only for monitoring. The reason is not convenience: acting weekly on small numbers keeps restarting the learning period, and then you are measuring your own restlessness rather than the campaign.

On small budgets there is a second reason — a week simply does not contain enough conversions to tell anything apart. The maths is in the small-budget guide.
