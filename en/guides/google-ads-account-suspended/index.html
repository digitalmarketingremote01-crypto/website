Google Ads account suspended — why it happens and how the appeal works

By Mohammad Danyal Shahzad · Updated on 13 August 2026

A suspension stops every ad in the account immediately, and you cannot advertise from it until it is reinstated. Accounts are suspended for policy or Terms violations and for payment reasons — unpaid balances, chargebacks, suspicious payment activity, or a compromised login. You can appeal; for billing cases Google may require you to verify your payment method within 30 days.

A suspension usually arrives without warning and stops delivery at once — mid-season, mid-campaign. This guide sets out the documented reasons, the official appeal route, and the deadlines that actually apply. Everything here comes from Google's own help pages.

What a suspension actually does

Google states the consequence plainly: "All ads in the suspended account will stop running, and you will no longer be able to advertise from your suspended account until the account is reinstated." (Google Ads Help)

It applies to the whole account, not individual ads. Unlike pausing, it is not your decision — and unlike cancelling, you cannot simply undo it.

The documented reasons

The headline category: "Accounts may be suspended if we find violations of our policies or the Terms & Conditions." (Google Ads Help)

Alongside that, Google names payment issues — unpaid balances, chargebacks and suspicious payment activity — and the case where someone else has got into your account: "An unauthorized user can steal credentials and access your Google Ads account without your permission. If we detect that an unauthorized user is trying to access your Google Ads account, we'll temporarily suspend your account to protect against unauthorized charges." (Google Ads Help)

On the billing side, the trigger is described as "Unpaid balance or concerns about future payments" (Google Ads Help).

How the appeal works

Google provides for it explicitly: "If you believe there's been an error and that you haven't violated Google Ads' policies, submit an appeal." (Google Ads Help)

The entry point is the notice inside the account itself, not a separate form you have to hunt down.

The 30-day deadline nobody mentions

For billing and payment suspensions, the appeal depends on a verification step — and that step has a clock on it: "Before processing your Google Ads account suspension appeal for Billing and Payment suspensions, you may be asked to verify your payment method within 30 days." (Google Ads Help)

Only then does it move: "After your identity/payment method is successfully verified, your appeal will be sent for review." (Google Ads Help)

Missing that window does not destroy your right to appeal, but it stalls it — the review does not begin until the verification lands.

How to spot a genuine Google email

Suspensions are a favourite hook for phishing, so it helps that Google publishes which address the verification updates come from: "Google Payments (payments-noreply@google.com)" (Google Ads Help).

Practical rule: never enter payment details or verification data through a link in an email. Sign in to Google Ads directly and work from the notice you find there.

Prevention costs less than an appeal

  • Keep the payment method current; expired cards are a common source of unpaid balances.
  • Keep more than one person with admin access, so a lost or locked login does not freeze the account — see transferring ownership.
  • Prune access regularly: remove former agencies and staff.
  • When changing agencies, hand billing over cleanly, or unpaid balances pile up on the old setup — see switching agencies.
